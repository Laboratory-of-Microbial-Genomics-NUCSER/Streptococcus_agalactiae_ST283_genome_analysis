## Abstract
Streptococcus agalactiae ST283 is an emerging foodborne zoonotic pathogen linked to invasive disease outbreaks associated with freshwater fish consumption in Southeast Asia. In this study, we report the first isolation and genomic characterization of serotype-III-4 ST283 from the Indian aquatic system. To assess the genetic relatedness of the Indian isolate strains associated with human outbreaks and evaluate its zoonotic potential, we performed a comparative genomic analysis of 416 globally distributed ST283 genomes identified from a worldwide dataset of 24,747 S. agalactiae genomes. Phylogenetic reconstruction revealed two recently diverged epidemic ST283 clones, namely SG-2015 and HK-2021, which shared a common ancestor around 2004, were responsible for the 2015 Singapore foodborne outbreak and the 2021 Hong Kong surge in human clinical cases, respectively, with the Indian fish isolate belonging to the HK-2021 clade. Aquatic and human clinical isolates within the epidemic clades showed high genomic similarity, including conserved CRISPR spacer profiles, shared accessory genes, and low pairwise SNP distances, suggesting recent cross-host transmissions. A genome-wide association study identified enrichment of the human-associated scpB-lmb locus, prophage and associated hypothetical proteins among epidemic isolates, indicating their potential roles in transmission, adaptation and virulence in human and piscine hosts. Together, these findings provide new insights into the evolutionary dynamics and emergence of epidemic ST283 clones with higher zoonotic potential, underscoring the importance of enhanced genomic surveillance at the human-aquatic interface to mitigate the risk of future zoonotic outbreaks.
